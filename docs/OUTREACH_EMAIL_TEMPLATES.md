# Outreach email templates

Final Addendum §13. Three cold-outreach templates Joey can personalise
before sending. Each is a starting point, not a script — the goal is
to read like a brief, considered note from one human to another, not
a marketing blast.

## How to use these

- **Personalise before sending.** Replace every `[bracketed]` field
  and, where you can, add one specific reference to the recipient's
  organisation (a recent OFSTED grade, a published strategy, a
  programme they've launched). Generic notes get filtered.
- **Send from Joey's mailbox** (`joey@ambertraining.co.uk`), not a
  shared inbox. The reply rate is several times higher when there's
  a real person at the other end.
- **British English throughout.** The funding context is UK-specific
  and US-style copy (e.g. "leverage", "ROI" in headlines) signals
  this is a marketing email rather than a human one.
- **One CTA per email.** The ROI calculator is the only ask. No
  "book a demo OR reply OR follow us on LinkedIn" trios — those
  dilute the response rate.
- **Plain text in the actual send.** No images, no logos, no signature
  blocks fancier than a name + role + URL. Rich HTML triggers spam
  filters and reads as a campaign rather than a personal note.
- **Reply with substance, not pitch decks.** When someone replies to
  any of these, the answer is a short follow-up with one specific
  question — never an attached deck on the first reply.

The calculator URL itself is:

```
https://esol.ambertraining.co.uk/roi-calculator
```

The onboarding-email path (Final Addendum §13, prior task) appends
`?org_name=…&org_type=…` for prefill. For cold outreach the bare URL
is fine — recipients haven't shared their details yet, and a
prefilled URL with an org name they didn't share themselves would
feel intrusive.

Best,
Joey

## Template 2 — FE college vice principal

**To:** Vice Principal (Curriculum / Adult Skills) at a further-education college
**Subject:** Adult ESOL throughput at [college name] — a quick question

Hi [first name],

I've been reading [college name]'s most recent strategic plan —
particularly the section on widening participation in adult
skills — and wanted to share a piece of work that may be useful
in the context of your ESOL provision.

You'll know better than I do that ESOL is a tough programme to
run profitably under the current ASF settlement: the per-learner
rate doesn't quite stretch to cover the small-group contact hours
the framework expects, and qualified ESOL teachers are
genuinely difficult to recruit anywhere outside London. Most VPs
I speak to have an ESOL waiting list they can't clear without
hiring staff they can't fund.

Project Silk is Amber Training's response to that squeeze. It
keeps qualified teachers in charge of the pedagogy — they review
AI tutor sessions, set Stage 3 objectives, sign off RARPA stages,
respond to safeguarding flags — but moves the between-session
contact hours onto an AI tutor that the framework recognises as
guided learning. The arithmetic is that one teacher can hold
3-4× the cohort they could under the all-contact-hour model,
which in turn lets you claim against the waiting list you're
currently turning away.

Try our ROI calculator: https://esol.ambertraining.co.uk/roi-calculator

It'll show you what [college name]'s annual unclaimed ASF figure
looks like at your current waiting-list size, and the payback
period against our platform fee. If it's worth a conversation
afterwards I'll bring the audit pack and we can spend half an
hour going through how the ILR exports look in practice.

Kind regards,
Joey

## Notes for future templates

- **Employer ESOL** (workplace English, often for hospitality or care)
  is a fourth audience the calculator supports via the
  `org_type=employer` option. The pitch is different — employers
  don't claim ASF, they pay direct — so the calculator's headline
  number is less useful and the conversation is about retention /
  promotion rather than unclaimed funding. A separate template is
  tracked as a Phase 27 follow-up.

- **Following up after a calculator submission** — when a prospect
  fills the calculator and shares their email, the admin Sales
  Intelligence page (Final Addendum §13, prior task) shows the
  submission with their unclaimed-income figure pre-computed. The
  reply email should reference that specific number, not a
  generic pitch. A "first reply after calculator submission"
  template is tracked separately.

- **OFSTED-grade-changed prospects** — when an organisation's
  OFSTED grade moves (good → outstanding, or requires-improvement
  → good), the trustees or governing body usually fund a
  programme review. A timely note in that window has a much
  higher reply rate than a routine cold outreach. Tracked as a
  Phase 27 follow-up.
